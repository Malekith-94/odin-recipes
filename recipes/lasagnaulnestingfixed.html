      • Mozzarella Cheese – 4 cups shredded (or more — this is the stretchy magic)
      • Parmesan Cheese – 1.5 cups grated (mix into layers and sprinkle on top)
      • Ricotta Cheese – 2 cups (for creamy texture between layers)
      • Egg – 1 (mix with ricotta for structure)
      • Fresh Parsley – ¼ cup chopped (mixed into ricotta layer)

      • Lasagna Sheets – 16 sheets (you'll need about 3–4 sheets per layer, depending on pan size; oven-ready or boiled)

      • Red Wine – ½ cup (added to meat sauce for richness, simmered down)
      • Spinach (fresh or frozen) – 1 cup chopped (added to ricotta mix for some green)
      • Crushed fennel seeds – ½ tsp (enhances the sausage flavor)
      • Fresh basil leaves – handful (between layers or on top for garnish)
      • Butter – a few pats on top before baking, for browning and flavor

      Assemble all the ingredients listed earlier. This includes:

        your ground beef
        Italian sausage (if using)
        onions
        garlic
        olives oil
        tomato products
        lasagna sheets
        cheeses
        any extra add-ins like spinach or red wine.

      Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C) so it's ready when you’re done assembling.


        Finely chop 1 large yellow onion and mince 6 cloves of garlic.

      In a medium bowl, combine:

        2 cups of ricotta cheese
        1 beaten egg
        1/4 cup of chopped fresh parsley

      Mix until smooth; this will be your creamy layer between the hearty meat and saucy layers.


      ---
        In a large, deep skillet or Dutch oven, heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil over medium heat.
        Add the chopped onion and minced garlic. Sauté for about 3–4 minutes until soft and fragrant.
        Add 2.5 lbs of ground beef (and, if using, 1 lb of Italian sausage) to the skillet. Brown the meat thoroughly, breaking it up into small pieces as it cooks. Drain any excess fat if necessary.
      • Stir in 1 can (6 oz) of tomato paste. Then add 2 cans (28 oz each) of canned crushed tomatoes and 1 can (15 oz) of tomato sauce.

      * Season your sauce by adding 1 tablespoon of sugar (to balance the acidity), 2 teaspoons each of dried oregano and dried basil, ½ teaspoon of crushed red pepper flakes (if you like a little heat), and a bay leaf

      For an extra rich flavor, pour in ½ cup of red wine if you have it handy.

      • Let the sauce come to a simmer. Reduce the heat and allow it to simmer gently for about 25–30 minutes, stirring occasionally. Just before you move on to layering, remove the bay leaf.
      • Lightly coat a 9x13-inch baking dish with a bit of the meat sauce.
        First Layer: Lay down a thin layer of sauce. Place a layer of lasagna sheets (approximately 3–4 sheets, depending on your pan and the shape of your noodles).
        Second Layer: Spread a generous amount of the meat sauce over the pasta. Add dollops of your ricotta mixture (use about 1/3 to 1/2 cup spread evenly), then sprinkle a mix of cheeses—start with about 1–1.5 cups of shredded mozzarella and a handful (roughly 1/3 cup) of grated Parmesan.

      If you’re including spinach or any extra herbs, now’s a good time to sprinkle them over.

      • Repeat the layering process: pasta, meat sauce, ricotta mixture, and cheeses.

      Aim for about 3 to 4 full layers, ensuring each layer is generous enough to carry the flavor throughout the dish.

      • On the final top layer, finish off with a thick layer of meat sauce and a generous blanket of mozzarella (about 1–2 cups) and a sprinkling of Parmesan to help form that delicious, golden, bubbling crust.
        Cover your baking dish tightly with aluminum foil (tip: lightly spray the underside of the foil with cooking spray to prevent sticking).
        Place the dish in your preheated oven and bake for 25 minutes with the foil on.
        After 25 minutes, carefully remove the foil and continue baking for an additional 20 minutes or until the cheese is bubbly and lightly browned on top.

      Once baked, let your lasagna stand for about 10–15 minutes. This resting time helps the layers set, making it easier to serve up neat portions.

      * Garnish with fresh basil leaves if you fancy, and then slice it up to serve to your hungry crowd.
