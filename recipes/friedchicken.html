Main Ingredients (Serves 8 Generously)

    Chicken – 4 lbs, bone-in, skin-on (a mix of drumsticks, thighs, wings, and breasts)
    Buttermilk – 4 cups (for soaking and tenderizing)
    Hot Sauce – 1/4 cup (adds flavor to the soak)
    All-Purpose Flour – 4 cups (for dredging)
    Cornstarch – 1 cup (for extra crunch)
    Salt – 2 tablespoons
    Black Pepper – 2 teaspoons
    Garlic Powder – 2 teaspoons
    Onion Powder – 2 teaspoons
    Paprika – 2 teaspoons
    Cayenne Pepper – 1 teaspoon (optional, for a spicy kick)
    Oil – Enough for deep frying (peanut or vegetable oil preferred)

Optional Extras

    Fresh rosemary or thyme sprigs – tossed into the oil for aromatic flavor
    Pickle juice – swap in ½ cup for some of the buttermilk for a Southern zing

Preparation

Brine the Chicken:

In a large bowl, combine 4 cups buttermilk with 1/4 cup hot sauce. Submerge the chicken pieces, cover, and refrigerate for at least 6 hours or overnight for best flavor and tenderness.


Season & Dredge:

In a separate large bowl, mix together 4 cups flour, 1 cup cornstarch, salt, black pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, paprika, and cayenne. Remove chicken from the buttermilk, allowing excess to drip off, and coat thoroughly in the seasoned flour mix. Press it in good for that crunchy crust.


Time to Fry

Heat the Oil:

In a deep skillet or Dutch oven, heat oil to 350°F (175°C). Use a thermometer to keep it steady—too hot and the outside burns before the inside cooks.

Fry the Chicken:

Carefully lower in the chicken pieces a few at a time. Don’t overcrowd. Fry in batches, turning occasionally, until golden brown and the internal temp hits 165°F (about 12–15 minutes for dark meat, 8–10 for smaller pieces).

Drain & Rest:

Remove chicken and set it on a wire rack over a baking sheet. Sprinkle with a pinch of salt while hot. Let it rest 5–10 minutes to seal in that juiciness.

Serving

Serve with hot biscuits, coleslaw, mashed potatoes, or just straight off the plate with sweet tea and a smile. This fried chicken’s so good, it'll have folks talkin’ about it long after the meal’s done.
